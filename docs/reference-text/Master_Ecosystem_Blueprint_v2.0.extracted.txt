1. Purpose and Scope
This document is the single authoritative blueprint for the Kenya Motorcycle Spare Parts & Supply Chain Ecosystem. It defines
the product, users, business processes, information architecture, security model, AI capabilities, integrations, notifications,
reporting, and operational standards required to build a production-grade platform.
The ecosystem connects motorcycle spare-parts manufacturers, exporters, importers, wholesalers, retailers, mechanics,
riders, and end customers in one trusted digital environment. Its purpose is to make parts easier to find, stock easier to
manage, trade easier to complete, money easier to control, and supply-chain decisions easier to make.
This blueprint is self-contained. It does not depend on interpretation of another document. Where earlier concepts are retained,
they are restated here as definitive requirements. The implementation roadmap is provided in a separate companion
document, but this blueprint remains the source of product and architectural truth.
1.1 Product definition
The platform is a multi-tenant, offline-capable, AI-assisted commerce and supply-chain operating system for the motorcycle
spare-parts market. It provides each business with private tools for selling, stock control, procurement, fulfilment, finance,
compliance, and customer communication, while enabling controlled discovery and trade across the wider network.
1.2 In scope
  Multi-tenant business accounts, staff roles, branches, warehouses, and device management.
 Product catalogue, motorcycle fitment data, OEM/aftermarket cross-references, images, and part identification.
 Offline-capable POS, quotations, sales orders, invoices, receipts, returns, credit notes, and customer credit.
 Inventory control, stock movements, transfers, counts, adjustments, reservations, batches, and stock valuation.
 Procurement, supplier management, purchase orders, goods receipt, landed cost, supplier returns, and reorder
intelligence.
 Wholesale operations, customer credit, dispatch, rider delivery, proof of delivery, and buyer-retention intelligence.
 Parts Radar reverse-search, verified supplier quotes, reservations, fulfilment, and compatibility assistance.
 Financial ledger, expenses, cash reconciliation, payroll preparation, commissions, payouts, taxes, and reporting.
 Configurable tax compliance, eTIMS integration, VAT calculation, electronic invoices, and compliance monitoring.
 WhatsApp Business messaging, in-app chat, notifications, alerts, reminders, and customer communication.
 AI agents for margin protection, inventory intelligence, part matching, communication drafting, fraud detection, and
customer success.
 Network marketplace services: verified profiles, blind-stock visibility, referrals, disputes, reputation, and settlement.
 Administration, analytics, reporting, integrations, support, security, and platform governance.
1.3 Out of scope at launch
  Full ERP manufacturing, production planning, and factory resource planning.
 Banking, lending, insurance underwriting, or credit provision by the platform itself.
 Autonomous execution of bank payments without explicit owner authorisation and a compliant payment provider.
 Public social-media features unrelated to commerce, fulfilment, or support.
 Unrestricted open marketplace listings without verification, moderation, and dispute controls.

2. Product Vision and Principles
The platform must feel simple to a shop attendant, powerful to an owner, reliable to a warehouse team, and indispensable to a
mechanic. It must continue operating when connectivity fails, protect commercial privacy, and make every shilling, unit, and
decision traceable.
2.1 Design philosophy
The platform follows the principle of “Ferrari engine, bicycle handlebars.” The user-facing experience must be radically
simple, fast, and chronological. The underlying engine must be deeply capable: multi-tenant, offline-resilient, event-driven,
financially rigorous, and AI-assisted.
2.2 Non-negotiable principles
  Trade continues offline. A user must be able to sell, check local stock, issue a receipt, record an expense, and queue a
part request without internet connectivity.
 One tenant, one secure boundary. Financial records, supplier costs, margins, customer lists, bank details, and internal
stock quantities are private to each organisation.
 The ledger is the source of truth. Every commercial and inventory event produces a balanced, immutable financial and
operational record.
 Speed is a product requirement. Core counter and warehouse actions must be achievable in seconds, with minimal
typing and large touch targets.
 AI assists; authorised humans decide. AI may detect, draft, rank, and prepare actions. Irreversible commercial, financial,
or external communication actions require explicit approval unless a narrowly scoped automation policy is enabled.
 Privacy enables participation. Businesses will share network-level information only when it creates value without
exposing competitively sensitive data.
 Compliance is configurable, not optional in governance. A business may operate in simplified commercial mode, but
the platform preserves a complete internal audit trail and can activate formal compliance without data loss.
 Every action is accountable. Sales, discounts, refunds, stock adjustments, approvals, exports, messages, and AI
decisions must be attributable to a user, device, role, and timestamp.
2.3 Success definition
The ecosystem succeeds when a retailer can complete a sale faster than a manual process, an owner can trust the profit
number, a wholesaler can fulfil orders reliably, a mechanic can find the correct part quickly, and a supplier can understand real
demand without accessing competitors’ private data.

4. Functional Capability Catalogue
The platform is organised into modular capability domains. Each domain owns its data, rules, workflows, notifications, reports,
and integration responsibilities.
4.1 Identity, profiles, and access
  Phone-number registration, OTP verification, passwordless login options, session management, and device trust.
 Business profile with name, category, location, contacts, operating hours, service areas, logo, photos, and verification
status.
 Staff accounts with role assignment, branch assignment, approval limits, cost visibility, and activation/deactivation.
 Device management: enrolment, device name, last sync, offline capability, remote revocation, and lost-device wipe.
 Customer, supplier, mechanic, rider, and partner profiles with relationship history and consent records.
 Permission matrix covering modules, branches, data sensitivity, approval thresholds, and export rights.
4.2 Catalogue and fitment
  Part master with canonical ID, name, description, category, brand, barcode/EAN, packaging, dimensions, weight, images,
and documents.
 SKU and variant management, including size, colour, quality grade, pack configuration, and unit of measure.
 Bike-model registry covering make, model, variant, engine type, year range, and regional naming variations.
 Fitment records linking parts to bike models, with OEM reference, position, side, compatibility confidence, and verification
status.
 OEM and aftermarket cross-reference mapping, including source, confidence, conflict resolution, and version history.
 Photo and voice intake for part identification, with AI-proposed candidates and mandatory human confirmation for uncertain
matches.
 Catalogue governance: supplier submission, duplicate detection, data-quality score, approval, publication, takedown, and
change history.
4.3 Inventory and warehouse
  Multi-location inventory across shops, warehouses, godowns, vehicles, and in-transit locations.
 Stock movements for receipt, sale, transfer, adjustment, return, damage, assembly, and count variance.

 Stock-transfer workflow with dispatch, in-transit status, receipt confirmation, shortage detection, and discrepancy
resolution.
 Barcode generation, label printing, shelf/bin location, pack breaking, and unit conversion.

 Customer credit with limits, outstanding balance, ageing, payment plans, reminders, and collection follow-up.

5.2 Event model
The platform should use an event-driven core. A business action creates a domain event, which updates projections, triggers
workflows, generates notifications, and produces ledger entries where financially relevant.
  SaleCompleted: updates revenue, stock, customer balance, cash/payment, tax, and reporting projections.
 StockReceived: updates stock, cost layers, supplier payable, and procurement status.
 StockTransferred: updates source and destination locations, in-transit stock, and audit trail.
 ExpenseRecorded: updates expense accounts, cash position, and profitability reporting.
 PaymentReceived: updates receivables, cash/bank, customer balance, and reconciliation status.
 PartRequestCreated: triggers matching, notification, and quote-collection workflow.
 QuoteAccepted: creates reservation, order, and fulfilment workflow.
 DeliveryCompleted: updates order status, rider earnings, COD, and customer notification.
 PayoutApproved: creates payable/settlement entries and unlocks execution through an approved provider.
 ComplianceSubmitted: records eTIMS status, invoice signature, and exception handling.

key.

 Duplicate submissions are recognised by idempotency key and return the original result rather than creating duplicates.
 Financial records use append-only journal entries; corrections use reversing entries.

6. Offline-First Architecture
Offline operation is a core commercial requirement, not a caching feature. The local device must be able to complete the full
transaction workflow and synchronise safely when connectivity returns.
6.1 Offline capability
  Cashier: complete sale, apply permitted discount, accept cash or mobile-money reference, issue receipt, record expense,
and check local stock.
 Storekeeper: receive goods, issue stock, transfer between local locations, record damage, and perform count.
 Mechanic: search cached catalogue and fitment data, create photo/voice part request, and queue it for broadcast.
 Owner: view cached sales, stock, expenses, and pending approvals; cloud-only analytics are clearly marked unavailable.
 Rider: view assigned deliveries, update status, capture proof of delivery, and queue updates.
6.2 Synchronisation model
  Use an outbox pattern: operations are written locally before UI confirmation, then synchronised with retries and dead-letter
handling.
 Use client-generated UUIDs and idempotency keys to prevent duplicate sale, payment, stock, or request creation.
 Use event ordering by device sequence and server receipt time.
 Use stock reservations at the point of sale to reduce overselling during offline periods.
 Use server-side conflict detection and a reconciliation queue for material conflicts.
 Use Background Sync where available, but never rely on it as the only retry mechanism.

7. Security, Privacy, and Tenancy
Security must protect against external intrusion, insider misuse, cross-tenant leakage, and unsafe AI access. The platform
must assume that no request, device, user, or agent is trusted by default.
7.1 Security controls
  Enforce tenant isolation at database, API, cache, search-index, file-storage, analytics, and AI-context layers.
 Use role-based access control plus attribute-based rules for branch, cost visibility, approval limit, customer ownership, and
time window.
 Separate duties: the person recording a sale should not automatically approve its refund, stock adjustment, or payout.
 Maintain immutable audit logs for authentication, permission changes, price overrides, discounts, refunds, stock
adjustments, ledger corrections, exports, and agent actions.
 Encrypt data in transit and at rest; isolate secrets; rotate keys; enforce device enrolment and remote revocation.
 Apply rate limiting, anomaly detection, login protection, session expiry, and secure password recovery.
 Maintain tested backup, restore, disaster-recovery, and business-continuity procedures.

13. Architectural Directive
Build the ecosystem as a trustworthy operating system for motorcycle-parts commerce. The local device must remain useful
when the network disappears; the cloud must provide intelligence, coordination, compliance, and network effects; and AI must
amplify trusted operators rather than replace financial accountability.
The foundational priorities are: canonical domain model, immutable financial ledger, explicit offline conflict policy, agent
governance, parts-fitment data strategy, notification architecture, reporting architecture, and phased rollout. With these
foundations, the ecosystem can become durable, secure, and commercially compelling for every participant in the motorcycle
spare-parts supply chain.
